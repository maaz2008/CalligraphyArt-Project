About Calligraphy

Calligraphy is the art of beautiful and decorative handwriting. The word calligraphy comes from the Greek words “kallos” meaning beauty and “graphein” meaning to write.

Arabic Calligraphy

Arabic calligraphy developed with the spread of Islam in the 7th century and became an important form of artistic expression. It was mainly used to write the Holy Qur’an and decorate mosques and manuscripts. Over time, various styles such as Kufic, Naskh, Thuluth, and Diwani evolved, each with its own beauty and structure. Today, Arabic calligraphy remains a respected and timeless art that reflects spiritual, cultural, and artistic values.

Greek Calligraphy

Greek calligraphy began around 900 BCE with the Greek alphabet. It started on stone and pottery and later developed into neat, decorative writing on books. During the Byzantine period, it became more artistic and was used mainly in religious and educational texts, helping preserve important knowledge.

Indic Calligraphy

Indic calligraphy originated in ancient India with the Brahmi script. It was used for religious texts, literature, and official records on stone and palm leaves. Over time, regional scripts like Devanagari, Tamil, Bengali, and Gujarati developed their own styles, focusing on balance and smooth flow of letters.
